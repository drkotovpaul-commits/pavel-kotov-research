Hypotheses

Developing propositions for empirical investigation.

This register records hypotheses and research propositions as they develop, together with their scientific status, measurement requirements and potential routes toward falsification.

Purpose

A hypothesis is a proposition to be tested, not a conclusion.

Hypotheses presented here are provisional scientific propositions. Their inclusion does not imply empirical confirmation.

A useful hypothesis should define a relationship that can be operationalised, measured and challenged by evidence.

The register therefore distinguishes conceptual plausibility from empirical support.

Active Hypotheses

Current propositions under development.

The hypotheses below represent the current conceptual layer of the research programme and remain subject to refinement, criticism and empirical testing.

H1 — Movement Organisation and Biological State

Measurable characteristics of movement organisation may be systematically related to the dynamic internal biological state of the organism.

Status · Developing

H2 — Temporal Relationship

Synchronously measured movement characteristics and multisystem physiological variables may reveal time-dependent relationships that are not captured by isolated measurements.

Status · Developing

H3 — Recovery Trajectory

Characteristics of post-movement recovery may provide information about the subsequent response trajectory within an individual.

Status · Developing

H4 — Repeated Exposure and Adaptation

Repeated exposure to a comparable movement stimulus may produce reproducible within-person changes in the characteristics of the multisystem response.

Status · Developing
Research Logic

Later propositions depend on earlier validation.

The research programme is organised sequentially. Claims concerning recovery, adaptation or downstream relevance require adequate validation of the preceding measurement and response relationships.

Movement measurement → Multisystem response → Recovery → Repeated exposure → Adaptation

This sequence represents a research dependency structure, not evidence that each relationship has already been demonstrated.

Falsifiability

A useful proposition must permit contradictory evidence.

A hypothesis should generate predictions that could reasonably fail. Findings that are compatible with every possible observation do not provide a meaningful test.

For the present programme, falsifiability requires operationally defined variables, appropriate temporal synchronisation, reproducible measurement and study designs capable of distinguishing the proposed relationship from plausible alternatives.

A hypothesis may therefore be revised, narrowed, rejected or replaced as evidence accumulates.

Hypothesis Status

Scientific status is recorded explicitly.

The register uses status categories to distinguish conceptual development from empirical evaluation.

Developing

The proposition is being formulated or conceptually refined.

Under Evaluation

Measurement definitions, assumptions or existing evidence are being examined.

Testable

The proposition has sufficient operational structure to support a defined empirical test.

Revised or Rejected

The formulation has been modified, narrowed or rejected following critical analysis or evidence.

Scientific Boundary

Hypotheses do not constitute established biological mechanisms.

The propositions recorded here are part of a developing research programme. They should not be interpreted as validated biological laws, diagnostic criteria or demonstrated clinical effects.

Their scientific value depends on whether they can survive increasingly rigorous measurement, comparison and empirical testing.
