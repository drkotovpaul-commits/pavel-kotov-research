Research Position

Independent scientific practice grounded in measurable questions.

My current work investigates relationships among the biological state of the human organism, movement-related load, movement organisation and the physiological response that follows.

The objective is to develop research questions and methodological structures that can be operationalised, measured and challenged through empirical testing.

Observed relationships are not treated as evidence of mechanism, causality, clinical effectiveness or preventive benefit unless those claims are independently supported by appropriate empirical evidence.

Research Areas

Interconnected levels of investigation

Biological State

Dynamic physiological conditions present before, during and after a defined movement-related exposure.

Movement and Load

Organisation and magnitude of movement-related demand, considered in relation to the state in which that demand is received.

Response and Adaptation

Multisystem response, recovery dynamics, repeated exposure and reproducible within-person change over time.

Research Methodology

Operational definitions, synchronized measurement, reproducibility, feasibility design and falsifiable research structures.
