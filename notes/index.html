Working Construct

State-Dependent Biological Load remains a candidate model.

State-Dependent Biological Load is being developed as a working construct for describing the internal biological demand associated with a movement-related exposure.

The construct assumes neither that external workload and internal biological demand are equivalent nor that a given external movement dose produces the same response in every biological state.

Its current candidate representation is multivariable and state-dependent rather than a single universal numerical score.

Whether this construct provides useful explanatory or predictive information remains an empirical question.

Observational Principle

Observation, measurement and biological interpretation remain distinct.

Movement characteristics can be observed and quantified, while their biological meaning remains dependent on measurements at the relevant physiological level.

A movement observation alone should therefore not be treated as evidence of cellular state, systemic mechanism, causal regulation or clinical effect.

Likewise, a change in one physiological variable should not automatically be interpreted as representing the state of the whole organism.

These distinctions remain part of the methodological architecture throughout the research programme.

Load and State

The same external demand may not represent the same biological demand.

A central methodological problem is how to distinguish the externally prescribed movement demand from the internal biological response generated under a particular current state.

External load may be described through variables such as duration, speed, mechanical demand, movement organisation or weight-bearing conditions.

Internal response requires separate physiological characterization and cannot be inferred from the external prescription alone.

The research question is therefore not only how much external work was performed, but how that demand was accommodated by the organism at that time.

Feasibility Development

First test the measurement problem.

Before testing adaptation, cellular regulation or long-term health relevance, the programme must establish whether the proposed state-dependent response can be measured reliably.

Standardized Exposure

Define movement-related demand precisely enough that comparisons across observations remain interpretable.

Baseline Characterization

Record the biological and functional state from which each exposure begins rather than assuming equivalent starting conditions.

Synchronized Response

Observe prespecified response variables within a common temporal structure during exposure and recovery.

Reproducibility

Determine whether observed response differences can be reproduced within the same individual before broader interpretation is attempted.

Experimental Contexts

Controlled manipulation and ecological observation serve different purposes.

A controlled locomotor context can be useful when the research objective is to manipulate a component of external demand while maintaining a more standardized movement exposure.

Natural-environment observation serves a different purpose: characterizing movement organisation under less constrained conditions that may better represent habitual gait.

These contexts should therefore not be treated as interchangeable. One may support experimental control, while the other may provide greater ecological information.

The methodological value of each context depends on the specific research question being tested.
