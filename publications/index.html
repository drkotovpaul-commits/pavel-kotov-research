Preprint Abstract

From movement organization to adaptation.

Regular physical activity is an established component of the prevention and management of noncommunicable diseases, while contemporary systems and Network Physiology approaches investigate coordinated responses across multiple physiological systems and time scales.

The present conceptual paper asks how the organization of movement relates to the dynamic multisystem response and subsequent adaptation of the whole organism.

The framework distinguishes movement organization from movement modality, external stimulus, functional performance, acute response, recovery and longer-term adaptation.

It proposes a temporally structured sequence connecting movement organization, multisystem response, recovery, repeated exposure, adaptive trajectory and health relevance.

The paper is conceptual and reports no original experimental data. Its purpose is to provide a falsifiable research framework through which the relationship between movement organization, multisystem physiology and whole-organism adaptation can be empirically investigated.

Framework Structure

Seven linked levels of the proposed research process.

The framework treats movement as both an observable expression of biological organization and a recurrent perturbation of the organism.

1. Current Biological State

The organism enters a movement episode with a dynamic internal state shaped by biological, environmental and contextual conditions.

2. Movement Impulse

A temporally bounded episode of movement used to produce and characterize a measurable biological perturbation.

3. Organization of Movement

Spatial, temporal, mechanical and neuromuscular characteristics of the movement episode.

4. Multisystem Response

A temporally characterized response profile involving prespecified variables from more than one physiological domain.

5. Recovery / Remodelling

Post-movement response and its temporal resolution, including recovery kinetics and transition toward a subsequent state.

6. Repeated Exposure

Repeated movement episodes used to determine whether response characteristics change systematically.

7. Adaptation

Reproducible changes in response characteristics across repeated exposure and their relationship to longer-term trajectories.

Research Propositions

Six propositions form a cumulative testing sequence.

P1 — Movement Organization → Multisystem Response

At a comparable external movement stimulus, systematic differences in movement organization may be associated with reproducible differences in multisystem physiological response.

P2 — Response → Adaptive Trajectory

Repeated exposure may be associated with systematic within-person changes in prespecified characteristics of multisystem response and recovery.

P3 — Incremental Information

Temporal characteristics of movement organization may provide incremental information beyond conventional activity dose, functional performance and isolated physiological measures.

P4 — Response-Guided Modulation

Response-guided modification of a subsequent movement stimulus may produce a distinguishable response trajectory compared with a fixed-stimulus approach.

P5 — Health Relevance

Validated movement-response trajectories may show reproducible associations with established physiological, functional or behavioural risk factors relevant to noncommunicable disease development.

P6 — Longitudinal Prevention

Future longitudinal studies may determine whether validated movement-response trajectories are associated with subsequent health or NCD outcomes.

P5 and P6 are explicitly downstream hypotheses and are not claims of the present conceptual paper.

Scientific Position

A framework for testing, not an established biological claim.

The paper does not propose a new biological law, diagnostic instrument or therapeutic modality.

Existing systems biology, integrative physiology and Network Physiology are treated as established scientific contexts within which the proposed framework could be evaluated.

Its proposed contribution is narrower: to connect movement organization with multisystem response, recovery, repeated exposure and adaptation in a common temporally structured research design.

The framework remains falsifiable. If reproducible movement-response relationships cannot be demonstrated, the framework must be revised or rejected.

Proposed Empirical Progression

From measurement feasibility to long-term relevance.

1. Measurement feasibility → 2. Acute response → 3. Recovery → 4. Repeated exposure → 5. Adaptive trajectory → 6. Health relevance → 7. Longitudinal prevention

The staged design intentionally separates mechanistic validation from clinical and preventive inference.
